Title: Tour Hydration
Date: 2026-07-24
Tags: water, gear, bicycle

I've gone through many iterations of my water carrying strategy for touring. This is the current state of things. It will probably continue to evolve.

On every bicycle tour I pack my [Sawyer Squeeze filter and accouterments](/2019/07/sawyer-squeeze/). Thus a resupply may be natural (a creek, a lake, a trickle from a rock face, etc) or man-made (a cafe, a konbini, a water fountain at a park, etc).

I prefer to use an insulated, plastic bike bottle as my primary drinking vessel. I opt for the [Camelbak Podium Chill 24oz](https://www.camelbak.com/product/podium®-chill™-24oz-bike-bottle/CB-1873.html) with [Mud Cap](https://www.camelbak.com/product/podium®-mud-cap/CB-1947.html). A bike bottle is easy to drink from, both on and off the bike. Having something squeezable means I can also use it to periodically blast a little dirt off the bike. Something with a valve cover to keep the cooties off is key. Plastic insulated bottles are pretty poor at insulation when compared to double-wall metal bottles. This bottle won't keep cold water cold, but it will keep it cool for a bit, which is valuable on a hot day. I'm willing to sacrifice some of the internal volume for the poor insulation. Camelbak's Podium bottles have high-flow valves and (the post-2019 versions) are easy to disassemble for deep cleaning, so I tend to default to them whenever I need a plastic bike bottle (insulated or not).

I will always carry a bladder, as this is an efficient way to add more volume. And if I'm going to carry a bladder, I might as well pack one with a hose, even if the plan is just to use the bladder to refill the bottle. For the past couple years my preference here has been the [HydraPak Seeker 2L](https://www.hydrapak.com/products/seeker-2-liter-2021) with [HydraFusion Tube](https://www.hydrapak.com/products/hydrafusion™-tube). I typically use a frame bag, and this bladder will live in there, but if I am travelling framebag-less I can put the bladder in a fork bag or front pannier. The hose is secured somewhere in the cockpit using the [Camelbak](https://www.camelbak.com/product/magnetic-tube-trap™/CB-1256.html)/[Fidlock](https://www.fidlock.com/consumer/en/tube-clip/09721-p00002-blk) magnetic tube clip. The bladder usually wears the [Plug-N-Play 42mm Cap](https://www.hydrapak.com/products/plug-n-play-42mm-cap), which allows the hose to easily attach and detach. My filter kit includes the [28mm Filter Adapter](https://www.hydrapak.com/products/filter-adapter-28-mm), allowing the Sawyer Squeeze to output directly into the bladder.

Given my druthers, every evening of a bike tour involves a tea session. A tea session means a dozen or so steepings in [a titanium gaiwan](/2022/11/titanium-gaiwan/). Heating water for each steeping is a waste of fuel. Instead I will heat all the water at once, and store it in an insulated vessel to be decanted into the gaiwan when needed. Since water temperature is important here, I want a bottle with good insulation. For this I carry the [Hydro Flask Lightweight 24oz](/2023/07/hydro-flask-trail-series/) (which remains my EDC when not on tour). Generally I carry this bottle empty, stuffed into a pannier rather than keeping it full and accessible while riding.

Before starting the day's pedaling in the morning, I fill the Camelbak bottle and the bladder. If I am confident that resupply points will be plentiful, I'll keep the weight down by only putting 1 liter in the bladder. If I am unsure of the resupply opportunities, or I expect the day to be very hot, I'll put 2 liters in the bladder. Thus I usually start the day carrying 1.7 to 2.7 liters. If I need extra capacity I can also fill the Hydro Flask bottle. If I need still more capacity, the dirty bag in my filter kit is a [2L 28mm Vecto](https://minimalgear.com/collections/cnocoutdoors-collecting/products/cnocoutdoors-vecto-28mm?variant=40173968359513). Thus my total carrying capacity is 5.4 liters.

I can always stop by a store and buy a bottle or two of water if I need to bump that capacity up more. But if I know I'm going to have a 24-hour (or more) stretch without a water source, my preference is to pack a second Seeker 2L bladder. I prefer two smaller bladders over one larger bladder both for the redundancy -- if one bladder is punctured, I still have a second -- and for the greater flexibility in how the water weight is distributed around the vehicle.
